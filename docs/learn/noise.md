# Noise

A resistor at a nonzero temperature is not quiet: the thermal motion of its
carriers adds a small random current to the one Ohm's law gives. A circuit
simulator's noise analysis adds up such sources, each shaped by the
circuit's gain at each frequency, to predict the noise at an output. The
model's job is to say what each source is: where it sits, and its **power
spectral density** (PSD), the noise power per hertz.

Verilog-AMS has four noise functions for this (LRM §4.6.4): `white_noise`,
`flicker_noise`, `noise_table` and `noise_table_log`. They are added into
an ordinary contribution, and they are "only active in small-signal noise
analyses and return zero (0) otherwise". So adding noise to a model never
changes its dc or transient behaviour.

The `$strobe` shows the dc current is the resistive 1 mA alone: the
testbench evaluates a dc point, where the source reads zero.

The `//! noise` lines check what the device **publishes**. A testbench does
not run a noise analysis; a simulator does, and it needs from the device a
table of the sources and their densities at the operating point. Each
`//! noise` line names one row of that table, as
`kind(row,col)#source`, and may assert its fields: `name=` the label (the
optional string argument), `white=` the density in A²/Hz. Each prints a
`got=`/`want=` line with its verdict, `ok=1` or `ok=0`. The wanted value
is derived by hand in the header, never copied from VerA's output.

## What a simulator receives

The generated device carries the table as `noise_gens`, one entry per
source:

```console
{{#include ../examples/noise/emit.out}}
```

`row` and `col` are the branch the noise current flows through, `kind` the
shape of its spectrum, `source` which generator it is (below), and `name`
the label a simulator uses to group its noise report. VerA labels every
`white_noise` row `thermal`: the kind is the shape of the spectrum, flat,
not its physics; `2qI` shot noise is the same kind with a different
density. The densities themselves come from a function, `noisePsd`, which
the simulator calls at the operating point, because a density may depend on
the bias. [Noise, AC and tolerances](../host/tables.md) is the simulator's
side of this.

## Bias-dependent noise: shot and flicker

A current crossing a junction carries shot noise, 2qI, and most devices add
**flicker** noise, whose density falls with frequency. `flicker_noise(pwr,
exp)` is a density of `pwr` at 1 Hz that falls as 1/f<sup>exp</sup> (LRM
§4.6.4.2). Both densities here depend on the current, so they change with
the operating point:

Two calls are two generators, so the two rows have different source ids,
`#0` and `#1`: their noise is uncorrelated.

## A tabulated spectrum

When a density is known only as measured points, `noise_table` takes
(frequency, power) pairs and interpolates linearly between them (LRM
§4.6.4.3); `noise_table_log` interpolates in the logarithms, which draws
straight lines on a log-log plot (LRM §4.6.4.4). The pairs may come in any
order, since "the simulator shall internally sort the pairs into
ascending frequency":

so the simulator evaluates the spectrum at its own frequencies.

## Correlated noise

"Each noise function generates noise which is uncorrelated with the noise
generated by other functions" (LRM §4.6.4.6). To make two branches carry
the *same* noise, call the function once, keep its result in a variable,
and contribute the variable to both:

```verilog
{{#include ../examples/noise/correlated.va}}
```

```console
{{#include ../examples/noise/correlated.out}}
```

Both rows carry source `#0`: one generator, seen through two branches with
different gains. The second row's density is four times the first because
its coefficient is 2, and a density scales with the square of its
coefficient. `corr=1:1` asserts the correlation coefficient between row 0
and row 1: +1, since the two coefficients have the same sign. A
coefficient of opposite sign would make it −1.

## Exercises

1. LRM §4.6.4.6's second example builds *partially* correlated noise from
   three generators: `n1` and `n2` used once each, and `n12` shared by both
   branches. Write it with `corr = 0.25` and a coefficient of 1e-9 on each
   branch. How many rows does the table have, which share a source, and
   what is each density?
2. From those rows, what is the correlation coefficient between the total
   noise currents of the two branches?

<details>
<summary>Solutions</summary>

1. Four rows: each branch has its own generator and the shared one. The two
   `n12` rows share a source id. VerA numbers generators in the order the
   contributions reach them, so `n1` is `#0`, `n12` `#1` and `n2` `#2`. The
   unshared rows have density (1e-9)² · 0.75 = 7.5e-19 A²/Hz and the shared
   ones (1e-9)² · 0.25 = 2.5e-19 A²/Hz:

```verilog
{{#include ../examples/noise/partial.va}}
```

```console
{{#include ../examples/noise/partial.out}}
```

2. Only the shared generator contributes to both branches, so the
   covariance of the two totals is the `n12` density, 2.5e-19, and each
   branch's variance is 7.5e-19 + 2.5e-19 = 1e-18. The correlation is
   2.5e-19 / 1e-18 = 0.25, which is the `corr` parameter: that is what the
   LRM's construction is for.

</details>
